Does the port broker work in a browser?

Every tab now holds a MessagePort straight to the one worker that owns the database. A port cannot cross a BroadcastChannel, so a small SharedWorker carries it: a tab mints a channel, sends one end through the broker to whichever tab holds the worker, and that tab passes it into the worker. No OPFS is involved in the brokering, which is why it works in a browser that refuses OPFS inside a SharedWorker.

The broker also carries the succession notice. A Web Lock wakes the next waiter and nobody else, so when a successor takes over it registers here and the broker tells every other connection to reconnect. That says somebody else is serving, never that the hearer leads.

The test suite proves the relay in Node, where a port really is transferred but the broker runs in-process. What only a browser can answer is whether a port survives the SharedWorker boundary into another document's worker and still answers there. This page uses the shipped broker, not a copy of it. The decisive row is the third.
